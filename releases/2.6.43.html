You can now stop your scripts or log your client out from the Microbot dashboard, from your phone or any other browser. When you log out that way, or a script logs out on purpose, the break handler no longer logs you straight back in. Quest Helper on a free-to-play account no longer gives up when a quest needs more items than you have Grand Exchange slots, and the walker notices when a teleport from your worn jewellery didn't go through. It ships Microbot client 2.6.43 together with Slayer 1.1.5 from the Hub.

Stop scripts and log out from the dashboard

Say you leave a script running and head out. Halfway through the afternoon you check dashboard.microbot.cloud on your phone and see your character has been standing in the same spot for twenty minutes. Until now all you could do was look. Now you can act on it.

Open your client on the dashboard. Its details now have two new buttons:

  • Stop scripts stops every Hub script that's running on that client, the same as switching them off in the plugin list yourself. You can use it while at least one script is running.
  • Log out stops your scripts first and then logs the account out. You can use it while the client is in game.

Each button asks you to confirm. The dashboard then waits for your client to answer, which can take up to about a minute, and tells you whether it worked. On the client, a game message says what happened, so you know why your scripts stopped when you come back:

  • "The Microbot dashboard stopped your scripts."
  • "The Microbot dashboard is logging this account out."

After a dashboard logout, AutoLogin and the break handler stay out of the way, so the account stays logged out until you log in yourself or start a script in the client. More on that in the next section.

Don't want the dashboard to control this client?

Open the Microbot settings and untick Allow dashboard control under General. It's on by default. With it off, the dashboard can still show you the client but the two buttons are greyed out, and any command that does arrive is refused. Like the rest of the dashboard, this only works for a client linked to your Microbot account.

A logout now stays a logout

Say a script runs out of supplies and logs out on purpose, or you press Log out on the dashboard. If a break was already running at that moment, the break handler used to log the account straight back in when the break ended. Break handler v2 could also mistake the logout for a disconnect and log you back in right away.

Both break handlers now respect a deliberate logout:

2.6.42

The script logs out on purpose. When the break ends, or v2 thinks you were disconnected, the break handler logs you back in.

2.6.43

The break handler waits. The account stays logged out until you log in yourself or start a script in the client.

Once you log back in by hand, the break handler picks up where it was and finishes the break as usual. Without a deliberate logout nothing changes: breaks and play schedules log you in as before, and if the game disconnects you, break handler v2 still logs you back in. Break handler v1 is now 2.1.3 and v2 is 2.0.13.

Quest Helper buys items in batches

With Obtain Missing Items turned on, Quest Helper buys the quest items you don't have at the Grand Exchange. If a quest needed more items than you had free Grand Exchange slots, it stopped with "Not enough free Grand Exchange slots for missing quest items (need 6, have 3)". A free-to-play account only has 3 slots, so for most quests the feature only worked on members accounts.

Now it buys in rounds. Say your free-to-play account is missing 6 items for a quest. Quest Helper buys the first 3, collects them, then buys the other 3:

  1. It checks your gp first. Before buying anything, Quest Helper prices every missing item and adds up your coins in the bank and inventory. If you can't afford the whole list, it stops right away and says how much gp you need, so you're not left with half the items bought.
  2. It buys as many items as you have free slots. The rest are bought on the next round.
  3. It collects finished offers when every slot is taken. If your slots are all full but some offers have finished, Quest Helper collects them to free the slots instead of stopping.
  4. It stops cleanly if collecting doesn't help. If finished offers can't be collected, for example because your bank is full, it stops with "Could not collect finished Grand Exchange offers (bank full?). Collect them manually and restart." instead of trying again and again.

Teleports that don't happen

When your route has a teleport on jewellery you're wearing, for example an amulet of glory to Edgeville or a slayer ring to the Slayer Tower, the walker opens the Worn Equipment tab and uses the teleport. Sometimes the tab doesn't open. The walker used to count the teleport as done anyway, even though you never left.

Now the walker notices the teleport didn't happen. It tries another way, such as a teleport in your inventory, or tries again. The same goes for scripts that take off or use worn items: if the Worn Equipment tab doesn't open, the script is told the action failed instead of carrying on as if it had worked.

Plugin updates

Slayer1.1.5

If you stopped Slayer while it was walking to your task, or about to set up your cannon, the plugin could crash with an error at the moment its destination or cannon spot was cleared. It now keeps its own copy of where it's going for the rest of that step, so stopping it mid-trip no longer throws an error.

For script developers

Rs2Equipment.invokeMenu now returns a boolean. It returns false when the equipment tab doesn't open, when the item is missing, or when the action isn't in the item's menu. Rs2Equipment.interact(Rs2ItemModel, String), and with it every unEquip and interact overload, now returns that result instead of always true. Source that calls these methods compiles unchanged. A plugin jar that was already compiled against the old void invokeMenu and calls it directly needs to be rebuilt.

How to update

Restart Microbot to get client 2.6.43. Slayer 1.1.5 shows up in the Microbot Hub panel inside the client. To use the new dashboard buttons, make sure your client is linked to your Microbot account, then open dashboard.microbot.cloud.
